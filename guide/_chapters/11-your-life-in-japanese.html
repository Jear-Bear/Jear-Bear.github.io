The last S-tier piece of advice on my tier list is the “all the time” in All Japanese All The Time: slowly let Japanese take over your environment. The key word is slowly. It shouldn’t be like flipping a switch.

Switch things over gradually

Remember the phone story? Three months in, I switched my iPhone to Japanese because “it’s AJATT, everything has to be Japanese,” and immediately shared my location with my manager forever. Switch things when you can actually read them. Here’s roughly the order I went in:

  • YouTube’s interface language → Japanese. Follow Japanese creators so the algorithm feeds you more.
  • Music: find Japanese artists you love (easy rabbit hole).
  • Netflix and streaming profiles → Japanese audio and subtitles.
  • A Japanese Twitter/X or Instagram feed. Follow Japanese accounts, interact, let the algorithm do the rest.
  • Games in Japanese (chapter 6).
  • Phone and computer → Japanese, once you can read comfortably. Every button becomes mini input.
  • One hobby, fully in Japanese: recipes, workouts, a craft, whatever you do anyway.

Do your hobbies in Japanese

This is the advanced-stage secret. When progress stops being visible day to day, pursue the things you’d do anyway, but in Japanese. I cook from Japanese recipes and follow workouts in Japanese. When I have a weird question about kanji, I Google it in Japanese and read blog posts by natives. Slowly, your life converts. You stop needing English for anything except work and friends, which means more hours of immersion without trying.

Same with stories: enjoy the same series across mediums. Visual novel → manga → anime → (sometimes the unfortunate live-action). You already know what’s happening, so each new version is easier and you see the same words in different contexts.

Find the hours in a busy day

I work a full-time job, and the channel takes a lot of time too. I still get hours of Japanese in every day, because I stopped looking for “study time” and started filling gaps:

  • Lunch break: I drive five minutes to a Costco near work, open my laptop, put on headphones and watch Japanese YouTube with Migaku for an hour.
  • Commute: podcasts, or audio from an anime I’ve already watched.
  • Downtime at work: an NHK article, a few pages of a book on my phone.
  • At the gym: Japanese in my ears between sets.
  • Evening: one or two episodes of something easy, maybe ten pages of a novel. Mine five to ten words from whatever I watched.

Look for the moments you’d otherwise doomscroll. Even 30 minutes to an hour a day adds up to hundreds of hours a year. Then prioritize the rest of your life: sleep, friends, health. You don’t want Japanese to become the reason your life feels hard.

You don’t need to live in Japan

It’s a myth that you have to live in Japan to get really good. That used to be true, when the only way to get input was to be there. Now Japanese media has never been more accessible. With the internet you can build your own little Japan at home, tailored to your interests and your level, which you can’t do when the whole country is thrown at you at once. As a pretty socially introverted guy, I’d honestly rather have a mountain of input tools than be dropped in Japan with zero speaking experience.

That said, going is the best reward there is. On my most recent trips, speaking felt so much easier than a year before, and that was mostly because my vocabulary had grown from reading. Every hour at the desk shows up in those conversations.

試A trick for your first trip

Can’t read the menu? Look at the server and ask, おすすめは何ですか？ (What do you recommend?). You get a real conversation, they pick something great, and you never had to read a single kanji.

要TL;DR

  • Switch your world to Japanese gradually, in the order you can handle it.
  • Device language after you can read comfortably.
  • Do your hobbies in Japanese. Your life slowly converts.
  • Fill the gaps in your day instead of hunting for study time.
  • Japan is the reward, not a requirement.
