    The toolbox

    道具

    Anki, Yomitan, Japanese subtitles on anything, a reader for novels, a miner for games. The free setup I used for years, the paid one I use now, and how to set them up in one evening.

    12 min readBeginner✓ Read

    具

    Immersion only turns into progress if you can grab the words you don’t know without breaking your flow. That’s all these tools do. Set them up once, then forget about them and enjoy your stuff.

    There are two ways to do this: the free setup (Anki + Yomitan + a couple of add-ons), which I used for years, and Migaku, a paid all-in-one, which I use now. Both work. Pick one.

    • Install Anki (free on desktop and Android; paid on iPhone)
    • Add the AnkiConnect add-on
    • Install Yomitan in your browser, with dictionaries + a frequency list
    • Make a “Sentence mining” deck and connect Yomitan to it
    • Get asbplayer + bookmark Jimaku for Japanese subtitles
    • Mine your first card from something you actually like

    Anki: the flashcard app that remembers for you

    Anki is an SRS, a spaced repetition system. Think of it as Quizlet on steroids: it shows you each card right before you’d forget it. Get a card right and the gap grows (2 days, a week, a month…). Get it wrong and it comes back soon. It’s like watering a plant at just the right time instead of flooding it with gallons of water.

    Out of the box Anki looks really bare-bones. That’s fine. You’ll mostly see it for ten or twenty minutes a day.

    The card format I use

    • Front: the sentence, in Japanese, with the target word highlighted.
    • Back: the word, its reading, the definition, and optionally a screenshot and audio from where you found it.

    Reading the whole sentence on the front is the important part. You’re not testing “what does this word mean in English,” you’re testing “do I understand this Japanese.” If you’d rather not build your own card design, the free Lapis note type is great and is what I use in my game setup.

    Yomitan: hover over a word, get its meaning

    Yomitan is a browser extension. Hold Shift and hover over any Japanese word on any website: you get the reading, the definition, the pitch accent, how common it is, and a button to turn that sentence into an Anki card. It’s the single most important tool in this whole guide.

    1. Install it from the Yomitan site and allow the recommended permissions (it needs to read pages and talk to Anki).
    2. When it asks about dictionaries, grab the recommended ones. Make sure you get JMdict (the main dictionary), a frequency list (so you know if a word is worth learning), and a pitch accent dictionary.
    3. In settings, I like to turn on nested popups, so you can hover a word inside a definition and get a popup in your popup.
    4. Install AnkiConnect in Anki (Tools → Add-ons → Get add-ons → paste the code), restart Anki, then in Yomitan go to Anki → Enable integration. It should say “Connected.”
    5. Under Configure Anki card format, pick your sentence mining deck and match the fields: {sentence} → Sentence, {expression} → Word, {glossary} → Definition. Use {reading} if you want the word in hiragana.

    Now hover a word, hit the green +, and the card is in Anki. If you’ve already mined that sentence or word, the buttons turn purple so you don’t add duplicates.

    試Later: go monolingual

    Once you know around 3,000 words, add a Japanese–Japanese dictionary in Yomitan and put it first. Reading definitions in Japanese is immersion too. Chapter 9 covers this.

    Japanese subtitles on anything

    Crunchyroll makes it literally impossible to turn on Japanese subtitles, and lots of sites don’t have them at all. Here’s the free fix:

    1. Find the episode’s Japanese subtitles on Jimaku. It has pretty much every anime and a lot of TV.
    2. Install asbplayer (browser extension).
    3. Drag the subtitle file onto the video, on any site.
    4. If it’s out of sync, pause on a line and use Ctrl + ← / → to snap the subtitles to the audio.

    Because the subtitles are now just text, Yomitan works on them. Shift-hover, mine, done. You can mine straight out of Hunter × Hunter on Crunchyroll.

    Japan-only Netflix

    Netflix Japan has tons of shows with proper Japanese subtitles. You need a VPN set to Japan to see that library (the free VPN Gate works). Heads up: using a VPN for this is against Netflix’s terms, so do it at your own risk. I made a video on the phone setup:

    Reading novels and visual novels

    • ッツ Ebook Reader opens EPUB files in the browser, so Yomitan works on them and it tracks your reading speed. I read most of my light novels this way, sometimes with the audiobook synced so the text highlights as it’s read.
    • Game Sentence Miner (GSM) + OBS grabs the text out of any game (visual novels, Persona, Cyberpunk, whatever) and lets you mine it with screenshot and audio. Full setup in chapter 6.
    • Physical manga and books: Migaku’s phone app can photograph a page and make the text hoverable.

    Migaku: the all-in-one (what I use now)

    Everything above is free and works great, but it’s a handful of separate tools. Migaku puts it all in one: hover lookups with pitch accent on Netflix, YouTube, websites and its phone app, one-click cards with audio and screenshots, its own SRS, and a starter course. It even tells you what percentage of a video you’ll understand before you watch it. I started on Yomitan and Anki and switched about a year ago because I wanted to spend less time on setup and more time immersing.

    It’s paid. If money’s tight, the free setup gets you 95% of the way. If you want to try it, my link gets you an extra free month (it supports the channel too).

    The little helpers

    • jpdb.io: look up any anime, manga, novel or game and see how hard it is, how many unique words it has, and its rating. I check it before starting almost anything.
    • Immersion Kit: search a word or grammar point and get clips of it from anime and dramas. Perfect for “how is this actually used?”
    • Toggl: a timer. I track my immersion hours with it. Watching the number go up is weirdly motivating.

    要TL;DR

    • Free: Anki + AnkiConnect + Yomitan, plus asbplayer + Jimaku for subtitles.
    • Paid all-in-one: Migaku.
    • Card: Japanese sentence on the front; word, reading, definition on the back.
    • Set it up once, in one evening, then go enjoy your stuff.
